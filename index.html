Backyard Landscaping Perth Explained

The first discussion should describe what the ground does now, not only the desired appearance. Landscaping West identifies bare sand, patchy lawn and sloping blocks as conditions that need a ground plan before visible finishes are selected. Include the fall of the block, access for people or vehicles, nearby trees, existing paving and anything that could affect excavation.

On a slope, useful levels may require soil to be cut from the high side and filled at the low end. Retaining then forms part of the drainage plan. Water can carry Perth sand from behind a wall, so drainage behind the wall matters alongside the chosen material. Some retaining walls need a building permit through the local council before construction starts.

Pavers lifting near a large tree may indicate roots beneath a thin base. Replacing the top surface alone may not resolve the underlying condition, so the project review should identify it before pricing.

Choose surfaces for how the yard will work

Surface selection should follow intended use, access and preparation. Brick, limestone and exposed aggregate are described for patios, paths, driveways and pool surrounds. Synthetic turf suits a front or back yard where avoiding mowing is important, while roll on turf is installed over prepared sand.

Lighting belongs in the early layout. Landscape lighting needs protected cable runs, and the work is easier under bare ground than after a path is finished. If the goal is an outdoor room, a shaded alfresco area can be planned on footings intended to avoid settlement in the sand. Shade, paving and a built in cooking area can then support sitting and entertaining.

Compare the starting condition with the proposed finish before approving the visible work:

  • A sloping block raises questions about cut and fill, retaining, drainage and council approval.
  • Bare sand or patchy lawn raises questions about soil preparation, turf choice and irrigation coverage.
  • Established trees near paving raise questions about roots and the base beneath the lifted surface.

Plan planting and water together

Perth sand drains quickly, so planting choices and irrigation settings need to be considered together. Landscaping West describes improved sandy soil, soil improver in garden beds and reticulation zones divided according to how thirsty the planting is. Native and waterwise gardens can use plants grouped by water need, while lawn and garden beds may require different treatment.

The two day watering roster is a design constraint. Water can move past shallow roots in fast draining sand, leaving plants dry even when the surface has been watered. Ask how soil will be improved, how sprinkler coverage will be checked and whether leaks or dry patches need repair before lawn is installed.

  • Confirm whether roll on turf or synthetic turf suits the intended maintenance pattern.
  • Ask how plants will be grouped by water need.
  • Identify missed sprinkler areas, leaks and dry patches before reticulation upgrades.
  • Decide whether mowing, weeding, mulching, hedging and green waste removal are occasional or recurring work.

Make the written scope useful

A written price should show what happens below and above ground. Ask for separate lines for preparation, materials, plants, reticulation, paving, retaining and structures. This lets you compare repairing a lawn with replacing it without treating the surface as the whole project.

Describe how the yard will be used, where access is available and what sits nearby. The published enquiry guidance asks for access details and nearby factors that may affect the work. It also says to mention when both design and construction are wanted so both can be considered in the scope.

The installation sequence depends on the agreed work. Ground preparation and levelling may come first, followed by retaining and drainage, paving and structures, then soil, plants and lawn. Reticulation is tested before the lawn goes down. Timing should be confirmed after the scope is set, particularly where a sloping block, retaining wall or alfresco area is involved.

Common questions

What should be discussed before hiring a landscaping company in Perth? Ask who handles design and construction, what the written price includes, how plants and lawn suit sandy soil and summer heat, and who handles a permit if a retaining wall is involved. Trade training such as Certificate III in Landscape Construction is also worth raising.

How long can a Perth landscaping project take? Duration depends on yard size and the work involved. Lawn and garden beds generally involve fewer stages than levelling a slope, building a retaining wall or adding an alfresco area. A wall requiring approval may add time before building starts.

What happens first during installation? Ground preparation and levelling come first, so clear access is important. The agreed sequence may then cover retaining and drainage, paving and structures, soil, plants and lawn, with reticulation tested before the lawn is laid.

What information helps produce a useful quote? Provide the suburb, a description of the yard, access details and nearby factors that could affect the work. Ask for preparation, materials, plants, reticulation and structures to be shown separately.

Do consumer rights apply to landscaping work? The published guidance states that Australian Consumer Law guarantees apply to a landscaping service paid for, and that a contract cannot sign those rights away.
